Home foliage — carrying the illustration's own frame down the page

The foliage is the hanging vines, monstera leaves and pink wisteria that frame the top corners of the hero artwork — not a landscape. Every mark below is redrawn from that artwork in the same flat-vector idiom and the same sampled colours, so the page has one illustration running through it rather than a photograph followed by a second drawing.

It hangs in the gutters, beside the cards rather than behind them. That is what lets it be drawn at a strength you can actually see: at the edges it is framing a figure, not competing with one. The ground under all five is the same agreed descent, so the only variable here is the foliage.

1 · Vine curtain
The hero's own top corners, repeated down both edges. Long vines with wisteria hanging between them — the densest reading, and the closest match to the artwork.
2 · Alternating corners
One cluster per section, swapping left and right down the page. The frame never sits on both sides at once, so it reads as punctuation rather than as a border.
3 · Continuous rail
One unbroken vine running the entire height of each edge, leaves and blooms along it. Literal continuity — the illustration never stops, it just gets thinner.
4 · Drift
Single leaves and single blooms, widely spaced and small. The quietest option — the foliage is a recurring note down the margin rather than a frame.
5 · Crowns and a close
Foliage at each section head — the page as chapters in a garden — plus a band of it across the foot, so the scroll ends on the illustration instead of running out on bare ground.
