ANTZ v4 — UI & UX improvement plan

UX audit & improvement plan

The Command Centre is a report wearing a dashboard’s clothes

V4’s module pages are the best work in the product. The home screen is not yet doing the job its own header comment claims for it: eight sections state the same twelve measures three to five times over, and the twenty-eight things that actually need a decision today begin two screens below the fold.

Subject antz-command-centre-v4 · localhost:5202 Reviewed 10 Aug 2026 Widths 402 · 1024 · 1512 Method live capture + measured DOM

Verdict

Do not redesign the module pages. Animal Population, Pharmacy and Mortality are genuinely strong: scoped figures, honest provenance, real drill-downs. The work is on the home screen and on the shared visual system that every page inherits.

Three problems account for almost everything else. The home screen repeats itself — food wastage is stated five separate times, six of eleven Executive KPIs reappear verbatim lower down. It inverts priority — the greeting and an illustration own the first screen while critical alerts start at 1,857 px. And it contradicts itself — the right rail says 14 critical alerts while the section header says 28 urgent. Fix those three and the page halves in length without losing a single fact.

What is already right

Listing this first because the plan below deliberately leaves it alone.

  • Module pages. One scope, applied everywhere; every figure recut to site and window; a population bridge that visibly closes. This is the standard the home screen should be held to.
  • Scope lives in the URL, above the router. The window and site survive navigation and can be pasted into a message. Rare, and correct.
  • Container-query tiering. Sizing off the content column rather than the viewport is the right call — a 1280 px desktop genuinely gives cards less room than a tablet landscape.
  • Entity routes. Giving sites, species and animals their own URLs turns a module-only product into something you can navigate by thing, not just by question.
  • The code explains itself. The header comments record the reasoning behind decisions, which is why this audit could argue with intent rather than guess at it.

Findings

Severity uses the product’s own vocabulary. Every measurement was taken from the running app at the three widths above, with reveal-on-scroll forced so nothing was mis-measured as absent.

Structure — the home screen

Critical
5×times food wastage is stated on one page

The same measures are restated three to five times, in three different visual languages

Executive KPIs, Trends, Zoo Health, Executive Health, Risk Indicators and the right rail are six different renderings of one overlapping measure set. Six of the eleven Executive KPIs reappear in Executive Health with a target attached; four reappear again in Trends with a twelve-month shape.

The reader cannot tell whether the second appearance is new information or the same fact re-lit, so they read all of it — and the page is long because it says everything three times, not because it covers a lot.

Critical
1,857 pxwhere Critical Alerts begins on desktop
2,395 pxand on a phone — 2.7 screens down

The urgent work sits below two screens of context

Missing animals, escaped animals, disease outbreaks, biosecurity incidents — twenty-eight items marked urgent — open below Executive KPIs and Trends. Above them, the first screen carries a greeting, an illustration and a total that moves by 181 a month.

The file’s own header lists “what needs my attention?” as the second question the screen answers. On the rendered page it is answered fifth.

Critical
14 vs 28alert count, rail vs section, same screen

Two numbers for one thing, visible simultaneously

The executive rail reads Alerts — 14 critical. The section header six hundred pixels away reads 28 urgent. Both are on screen together at desktop width. Lab Requests has the same split: 31 open in the rail, 4 of 31 in the alert tile.

This is the one finding that costs trust rather than time. A director who catches it once discounts every other figure on the page.

Eight sections, no way to jump, collapse, or reorder

The sidebar lists modules but not the home’s own sections, so the only way to reach Risk Indicators is to scroll past six others. Nothing is collapsible and nothing remembers what this reader actually opens.

Four of the eight names also collide: Health & Medical, Health Score, Zoo Health and Executive Health are four different things on one page.

The banner costs 576 px on desktop, 514 px on a phone

Greeting, org line, two filter pills, the total, and a full-bleed park illustration land before “Executive KPIs”. The illustration is beautiful and it is the single most expensive element on the screen.

It also continues behind the entire page — tree silhouettes sit under the Critical Alerts tiles and the Upcoming table, where they are texture behind dense data rather than atmosphere behind a welcome.

Visual system — inherited by every page

Critical
29distinct text colours in <main>
43distinct mark fills on the same page

Hue is used for identity and for severity at the same time

Vaccination is blue, Welfare magenta, Health Score crimson, Food Wastage brown, Tasks orange — decorative identity. Red and amber are simultaneously the status language on the CRITICAL / HIGH chips right beside them.

So a magenta meter next to a red chip encodes nothing consistent, and the calm the design is reaching for is spent on eleven hues that carry no meaning.

Critical
2.97:1#9b958b on white — AA needs 4.5:1
2.55:1the same colour on the sage ground

The second-most-used text colour fails accessibility everywhere it appears

FAINT #9b958b carries units, section asides, sub-labels, the sidebar’s zoo name, “Dispensed”, “BAND 8.2–9.8” — usually at 11–12 px, which makes it worse. It fails AA for normal text and also fails the 3:1 large-text floor.

MUTED #6d6860 already exists in the token set at 5.53:1 and looks nearly identical. ACCENT #2f9e5b is 3.41:1 — fine as a mark, not as text.

High
9×tiles whose delta reads “flat”

Three KPI anatomies in one section, and a delta column spent on non-information

Row one of Executive KPIs uses a sparkline, a pulse line and two bar sets; rows two and three use target meters; the wide Pharmacy tile carries no mark at all. Trends adds four more mark types. Measures of the same kind cannot be compared because they are not drawn the same way.

Nine tiles then spend their delta slot saying “flat”. Where nothing moved, the honest design is to show nothing.

Interaction & access

Critical
0:focus-visible rules in the codebase
63interactive elements in <main> on home

There is no designed keyboard focus state

Two focus:ring declarations exist, both on text inputs. Every card, KPI tile, alert row, approval tile and sidebar link falls back to the browser default — which several of them suppress with outline-none.

On a screen built for a director who will be handed a keyboard, tabbing is currently invisible.

High
25sub-40px tap targets on a phone
28 pxheight of the 7-day / 30-day chips

The primary controls are the smallest things on the screen

The date pill is 119×33, the site pill 99×33, the horizon chips 28 px tall, drill chevrons 14×14, sidebar rows 36 px. The floor for touch is 44 px, and the tablet layout is a touch layout.

Scope is the control the whole product hangs on; it should not be the hardest thing to hit.

Medium
5sidebar rows carrying a bare number

Sub-page counts are drawn exactly like alert badges

Animal Population 1, Preventive Medication 2 — these count child pages. In every other product a right-aligned number on a nav row means unread or outstanding, which is precisely the meaning this rail also needs for Alerts, Approvals and Tasks.

Medium
8 of 8sections gated on scroll

Reveal-on-scroll means the page cannot be captured, printed or searched whole

A full-page capture of the home screen returns section headers over an empty background; browser find-in-page misses anything not yet revealed. The motion is good and this is a real cost of it — content should mount and the reveal should be presentation only.

Layout

High
~800 pxof dead column on #/animals, desktop

The two-column masonry strands a column

On Animal Population the right column ends at Regulatory Standing while the left runs on through the class breakdown, leaving a tall empty band of ground beside real content. Any page whose two columns hold unequal content does the same.

Medium
identicalrail content on home and on every module

The executive rail is the same three cards everywhere

On the home screen it duplicates the middle column — Top Risks is Risk Indicators, Needs Attention is the alerts and approvals sections condensed. On a module page it is useful but static: reading Pharmacy, the rail still offers weather and staff attendance rather than anything about medicine.

What the home screen should become

Same facts, same modules, same design language. Four sections instead of eight, ordered by what a director does with them — and the first alert above the fold on a phone.

The plan

Five phases, ordered so each one is shippable on its own and the earliest is the largest win. Phases 3 and 4 touch the shared system, so they improve every module page for free.

One measure, one place — then reorder the home around the decision

Declare a single measure registry with, for each measure, its current value, its target, its twelve-month series and its severity. Every home surface reads from it, and each measure renders exactly once.

  • Retire Executive Health. Its six measures are the KPIs with a target attached — fold the target line into the KPI tile instead of restating the number.
  • Retire Risk Indicators from the column. It is the rail’s Top Risks with more rows; make the rail the one home for standing risk and let it expand.
  • Fold Zoo Health into the banner. Four indices beside the total answer “is anything wrong” where the reader is already looking.
  • Merge Critical Alerts and Needs My Approval into one Attention section, sorted by severity then age, and move it directly under the banner.
  • Derive every count from the registry so 14-versus-28 becomes structurally impossible rather than a bug someone has to notice.

8 sections → 4 · ~4,800 px → ~2,400 px · first alert above the fold on a phone

Largest change · no new data · no module page touched

Compress the banner; give the park a band, not the page

Keep the illustration — it is the product’s character and it should not be sacrificed. Cap what it costs.

  • Target ~300 px for the whole banner at every width, down from 576. Greeting on one line, scope pills beside it rather than under it.
  • Put the health score and the attention count beside the total, so the first screen answers “how big”, “how well” and “how urgent” together.
  • Stop the landscape at the banner’s lower edge. Flat ground under the data; the park stays a welcome rather than becoming a watermark.
  • Keep the scope pills pinned when the banner scrolls away — they govern every figure below them.

First screen: greeting, scope, total, score, and the top three things needing attention

Contained · banner and Landscape only

Make colour mean one thing, and make the small text legible

Two rules, applied through tokens so every page inherits them at once.

  • Hue encodes state, never identity. One accent plus the reserved good / warn / critical trio. Identity is carried by the icon and the label, which already exist on every tile.
  • Retire FAINT #9b958b as a text colour in favour of MUTED #6d6860 (5.53:1). Keep the faint tone for hairlines and de-emphasised marks, where contrast rules do not apply.
  • One KPI anatomy: label, value, unit, delta against target, one mark. Two mark types across the whole product — a line for a stock, bars for a flow.
  • Suppress the delta when it is flat rather than printing “flat” nine times; reserve the slot for movement worth reading.
  • Check #dc2626 on the sage ground — 4.15:1 is under AA for body text on the app’s own background.

29 text colours → ~8 · every text token passes AA · tiles become comparable

Shared system · improves all 22 module pages

Make it operable — keyboard, touch, and honest badges

  • One focus token, applied globally to every card, tile, row and nav link. Currently there is no designed focus state anywhere in the product.
  • 44 px minimum hit area on scope pills, horizon chips, chevrons and sidebar rows — through padding, not visual size, so nothing has to grow.
  • Reserve the sidebar badge for outstanding work. Replace sub-page counts with a disclosure chevron; let Alerts, Approvals and Tasks own the number.
  • Mount content, reveal on scroll as presentation only, so find-in-page, print and capture see the whole page.
  • Give the home’s four sections a jump control — with four rather than eight, a small sticky strip is enough.

Fix the column layout and make the rail follow the page

  • Stop stranding the second column. Either balance the two columns by measured height or give sections explicit spans, so no page ends with 800 px of empty ground beside live content.
  • Let the rail change with the route. Keep weather and activity global; swap the middle card for what the current module is about — low stock on Pharmacy, open cases on Health & Medical.
  • Remove the rail’s duplication on home once phase 1 lands, so the rail is the only home for standing risk rather than a second copy of it.

Open questions — your call, not mine

Is the home screen for the director, or for the board pack? The plan above assumes the director: attention first, performance second. If the screen also has to be the monthly report’s front page, Trends and Executive Health earn their place back and the answer is two views rather than one longer one.
How much of the illustration are you willing to lose? Phase 2 halves the banner. There is a version that keeps it at full height and puts the attention list beside it on desktop — richer, but it does nothing for the phone, where the cost is highest.
Should the module accent hues go entirely? Phase 3 says hue means state. The softer option keeps one hue per module as a small identity mark — the icon tint only — and bans it from every meter, bar and number. That preserves recognisability and still frees red and amber for severity.
Where does this land relative to the uncommitted work? The v4 worktree currently carries two sessions’ edits with nothing committed, and the existing handoff flags that as the thing to sort out first. Phase 1 rewrites Home.tsx substantially, so commit boundaries want deciding before it starts.
